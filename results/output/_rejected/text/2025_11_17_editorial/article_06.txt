Innovations in programme

In addition to shorter courses of treatment, such as BPaL, the government has also introduced other innovations, suchas Al-enabled hand-held X-ray devices that can detect even asymptomatic TB cases during community screening. This was used during the 100-day campaign launched by the governmentin December last year. In these active screening campaigns that began last year, more than 19 crore vulnerable individuals have been screened forTB, resulting in a detection of over 24.5 lakh cases, including 8.61 lakh asymptomatic cases. "This proactive approach draws on both global and local evidence underscoring the prevalence of asymptomatic TB in high-burden settings," the Union Health Ministry said in its statement. When it comes to nutrition support, the government hasincreased the direct benefit transfer sum from Rs 500 to Rs 1,000 a month per patient for the duration of treatment. Additional nutritional sunnort isalso provided by volunteers through the government's Nikshay Mitra programme. But, as DrShewade said, "there are still issues of delays and equity". "There is a need to ensure early - say within few weeks - transfer of first installment among the most vulnerable (those withsevere under-nutrition, BMI<16 and/or any other state specific vulnerability) before they develop unfavourabie outcomes," he said.

Growing at home not allowed Ifcannabis leavesarenot covered by the NDPS Act, why can one not grow aplant to harvest them? This is where last week's Kerala HC order becomes significant. Section 8(b) ofthe Act prohibitsthe cultivation of any cannabis plant except for medical or scientific purposes and in the manner prescribed by the government. The court noted that the definition of a "cannabis plant" in Section 2(iv) of the Act is "any plant of the genus cannabis". The court ruled that the statute doesnot distinguish between a plant with flowers and one without - the offence lies in the act of cultivation itself. In other words, the NDPS Act draws a clear line between the "cannabis plant" and the product ganja. While ganja is indeed defined by its flowering tops, the Act separately and expiicitiy prohibits the cultivation of any plant of the genus cannabis.
