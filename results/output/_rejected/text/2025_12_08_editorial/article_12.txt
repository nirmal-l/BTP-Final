THE EXPRESSION "Hindu rate of growth" reflected a colonial mindset that linked India's slow economic performance inthe decades after Independence to the faith and identity ofits people, Prime Minister Narendra Modi saidat an event on Saturday. How did the term come about? According to The New Oxford Companion to Economics in India, economist Raj Krishna coined itin 1982 as "a polemical device intended to draw attention to the meagre 3.5 per cent growth rate experienced by India over the long run. The fact that this rate of growth remained steady through changes in governments, wars, famines, and other crises, made it for him an inherently cultural phenomenon - hence the name." f ECON
