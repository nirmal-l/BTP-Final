HE CRASH ofa Tejas aircraft in an airshowin Dubai isunfortunate. The country lost a frontline pilotwho was doing his duty of showcasing our indigenous fighter. He would have been an ace aviator, as only exceptional ones are given display flying duties. He hasleft behind agrieving family and equally sad compatriots who would, asthis is being written, be doing what they canto console his near and dear ones. An international airshow setting is the worst place forsucha mishap - India was publicising theaircraft, several caineias weit recordiig, and our adversaries were waiching. That said, the show must go on. Four steps are essential. One, acceptance of the fact that in such high-visibility aircraft crashes, transparency in the public domain regarding the reason(s) for the accident and steps being taken to prevent further mishaps is vital to restoring public confidence. The authorities must release the accident investigation report, unlike the "mysmust release the accident investigation report, unlike the "mystery" that still surroundsthe Air India AH71 crash. Normally, the IAF'saccidentt investigation reports are confidential - an exception may have to be made in the Tejas case, as was done for the helicopter crash in which General Bipin Rawat lost hislife. Second, any negative impression of the operational capability of the Tejas needs to be put to rest. Humans were not meant to fly, so accidents, unfortunately, will happen. The government and the IAF have placed their trust in Tejas Mk1A and its successor, the Mk2, to be the backbone of the fighter fleet for the coming decades, thus emphasising that the aircraft meets IAF's capability requirements. To project the required deterrence posture, capability hastobebacked up with robust capacity building. To obviateany negativeimpression created in Dubai, Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd (HAL) must work overtime and meet itsdelivery promises so the combination of capability and capacity proves the naysayers wrong. This cannot happen overnight, but the ill effects ofthe accident must galvanise HAL. Third, the Dubai accident should not hold us back from publicising Tejas in future airshows. As someone who has worn the blues for almost four decades, this writer knows the inherent strength, professionalism, and resilienceofthe IAF's air warriors and their equipment. One Knows that, after the formalities are over, the first sortiewould be flown by the squadron's Commanding Officer - honouring a timeless Air Force tradition that the "old man" always leads from the front. The show will goon. Finally, the Dubai accident should be seen as just that - an accident A social media battle seemstobe going on about Tejas's capability. Toall those onsocial media, let me say: Trust the Indian Air Force. It will always do what's right for the country.
