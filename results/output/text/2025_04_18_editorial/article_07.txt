AS THE REPUBLICAN People's Party (CHP) was preparingtoconfirm its candidate for the next presidential election, Ekrem Imamoglu, the popular and charismatic mayor of Istanbul, whol had met during my tenure as ambassador to Turkey, was arrested and jailed oncorruption charges. Along with his close political associates and campaign organisers, hundreds of activists, students, journalists and others were also placed in detention. The police took him to Marmara prison just after he recorded a message speaking against autocracy that went viral, entrusting his fate tothe people. Sensing an opportunity, the CHP converted an internal party session into a voting process across the country, galvanising 15 million voters, or about 25 percent of the electorate, to nominate Imamoglu for the presidential election. Opposition parties have come together to call for economic boycotts. Wider protests have been organised toforce the government to release Imamoglu from what they call politically motivated charges. The ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP) remains steadfast in pursuing his prosecution and has denied charges of undermining the judiciary's independence. It has also forced Istanbul University to withdraw the degree awarded to Imamoglu, a prerequisite for a presidential candidate in Turkey, Recep Tayyip Erdogan has been in power since 2003; the younger generation has not seen any other leader.
