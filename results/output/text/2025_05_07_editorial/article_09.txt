The need for long-term health support is evident: "I have spent Rs 15 lakh on my treatment, but what] got wasjust Rs 1.5 lakh. If the government supported us properly, would have been standing today." This highlights the need for long-term and injury-specific treatment support for survivors, The experiences of affected individuals and families demonstrate the need for acomprehensive support programme that includes long-term health, education and livelihood support. Onesurvivorrecalled: "Immediately after the event, everyone, from family to relatives, helped. But if had received some support torun my family business or do any other job, would have been independent by now." Asin the case of the Pahalgam attack, the diverse profiles across states complicate the provision of support services, the possibility of mutual support and of their voices being heard. There is a great difficulty in navigating institutional processes, that too at a time of direneedand great personal tragedy. "Wedidn'tknow who to approach, or where to go for information from the government. If only someone had stood by us or turned back to see whether we were livingor dead, we would have feltmuch supported." These voices illustrate the need for better institutional mechanisms and access.

Huge variations across states, events and between survivors, in ex-gratia and welfare support (Rs 5 to 20 lakh for the deceased, Rs 50,000to 2 lakhs forthe injured and only one State offering jobs to wives of the deceased) highlight theconcem of lives being valued differently and undervalued across the states. Lately, several bills have been passed to enhance internal security. These narratives highlight the need for more attention to address the pain and suffering of terror survivors in the longrun. The current provisions for compensation and welfare measures are limited and inaccessible to most due to issues of limitation period, eligibility criteria and reliance on thediscretionary powers of officials. Thus, most survivors are left with no welfare support other than the one-time ex-gratia payment, which may not serve all well. The demand is for needs-andrights-based support. More survivor voices demonstrate the needtodraw lessons from these lived experiences in making the support programmes better. As one survivorsaid, "... do proper enquiry and identify the most vulnerable families and extend support See if there are old people, with only women in the family and no other earning member. How will their homes run without support?"
