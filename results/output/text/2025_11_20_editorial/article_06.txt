The Delhi government's proposal to allow private events, including weddings, at approximately 80 state-protected monuments to boost tourism has been met with cautious enthusiasm. Tombs, domes, and arches at the unlikeliest corners, bear witness to the histories that have built this city. Supporters argue that the proposal could foster deeper public engagement with these neglected sites. When the Delhi government organised a monsoon festival in Mehrauli, it drew attention to three obscure sites: Aam Bagh, Jahaz Mahal and Jharna. It also created a platform for local artistes to showcase their work. Cultural programmes at historic ruins are not unusual, like the Forever Is Now art festival at the Giza pyramids in Egypt and the Baalbeck International Festival in Lebanon. In India, Mehrangarh Fort and Gwalior Fort host music and cultural performances. These events have raised legitimate questions around conservation and the preservation of cultural memory.
