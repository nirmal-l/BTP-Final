From drone mapping to removing water hyacinth, how a river was revived in UP

TILL EARLY February, there was no visible sign of the Noon river that once meandered through Kanpur district's Rampur Narua village. Locals say an infestation of the jal kumbhi (water hyacinth), which affects water flow and aquatic life, had rendered the thriving river indistinguishable from the fields it once

snaked through. At Rampur Narua, its origin point, today, the river flows nearly six feet wide - the result of a state-wide river rejuvenation programme as part of which every district in the state has been tasked with reviving at least one river in their jurisdiction. In Kanpur, the district administration decided to revive the Noon, one of the district's four forgotten rivers, since it had been blocked due to the jal kumbhiand construction debris, and had not dried up like the others.
