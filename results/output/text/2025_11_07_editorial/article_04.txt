Later, Nehru developed a distinct vision for India's cosmopolitan cities such as Bombay and Madras. He saw them as polyglot zones whose vitality came from linguistic and cultural diversity. His reluctance about the reorganisation of states stemmed from a fear that linguistic nationalism would erode this cosmopolitan spirit. The unapologetic celebration of polyglotness was a quintessentially Nehruvian hallmark. Nehru's relationship with American leaders and geopolitics was often testy.
