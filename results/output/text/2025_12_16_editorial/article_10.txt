USIC HAS a way ofabsorbing departures, even of its most beloved custodians. Concert calendars fill up, other talented artistes come forward, andthe world settlestoa new rhythm. Lossisacknowledged, then folded into continuity. Yet the past year, marked by the absence of tabla giant Ustad Zakir Hussain, has shown that there are exceptionsto this rule. A year after his suddendemiseat 73, the limits of music's ability to absorb griefhave become apparent. Perhaps because Hussain was never only abrilliantartiste, never confined just to the stage, studio, or classroom. His presence extended beyond performances into something more elemental. Through his career, Ustad Allah Rakha Qureshi's eldest son - with his curly locks and disarming smile - stood in as the meaning of rhythm for India, a reference point through which audiencesand artistes understood taalas a living force. Itis rare fora musician rooted in the complex worlds of raga and riyaaz to be both popular with the masses and revered by purists. But Hussainoccupied that spaceeffortlessly.
