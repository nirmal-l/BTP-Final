STOP THE BULLDOZER

Supreme Court has, again, stood up against state arbitrariness. Its message must be heeded N RECENT TIMES, across states - and under governments across political lines - [= authorities have bulldozed two fundamental principles of justice. The first is that every person is innocent until proven guilty, and can be punished only after due process. Second, there can be no guilt by association, no collective punishment. By demolishing the homes of those accused of crimes and sometimes even those of their family members, governments have also shown acomplete disregard for the Constitution's letter and spirit, its guarantees of fundamental freedoms and protections against state arbitrariness. That the bulldozer disproportionately targets vulnerable communities and that the "instant justice" it purportedly provides is more akin to vigilantism than state action has also become a part of poll campaigns. In this context, the order by the Supreme Court bench comprising Justices AS Oka and Ujjal Bhuyan must be heard by executive authorities across the country and its message heeded. While ordering the Prayagraj Development Authority (PDA) to pay Rs 10 lakh each to those affected by demolitions it carried out in 2021, the Supreme Court said, "These cases shock our conscience. Residential premises of the appellants have been highhandedly demolished... Carrying out demolition in such amanner shows insensitivity on the part of the statutory development authority." It questioned the UP government's claim that notices were served on time. The Court's rebuke is of a piece with what it stated in November last year, calling the sight of a bulldozer demolishing a building "chilling", and saying that it "reminds one of alawless state of affairs". Bulldozers were used in contravention of due process first in Uttar Pradesh, then across several BJP-ruled states and more recently by the AAP governmentin Punjab. This selective demolition of "illegal" properties flies in the face of the Supreme Court guidelines laid down in November last year. The fine of Rs 10 lakh, the Court said, is meant to ensure that the PDA "will always remember to follow due process". Itis reassuring that the SC has stayed with the issue, and spoken up forthose at the receiving end of bulldozer injustice, affirming once again that it is the final port of call for citizens whose rights are violated by agencies of the very governments meant to protect them. It must continue to be vigilant and to hold authorities to account. Too many governments and political parties have come to celebrate the bulldozer and its short-circuiting of due process. The judiciary has a record of standing up forthe most vulnerable. It's time the executive and and political class did so too - without being threat-ened with fines or strictures.
