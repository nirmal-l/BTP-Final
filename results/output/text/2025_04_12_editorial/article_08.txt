IT BEGINSINNOCENTLY enough. A quick scroll while you wait for your coffee. A video of a stranger wiping down glass with a $40cleaning spray in a beige kitchen. A 22-year-old woman - sunkissed, softly lit - holding upa lip gloss and whispering, "This changed my life." A young man in tech explaining how he doesn't start his day without his $200 noise-cancelling earbuds, almond milk, and productivity stack. What was oncea casual feedof filtered breakfasts and throwback Thursdays is nowa theatre of aspiration. And the ticket to entry? Your wallet Instagram and its ecosystem of influencers didn't just change how we consume; it changed what we associate with consumption. Where traditional advertising shouted, Instagram murmurs. It slips under the skin. These aren't faceless corporations telling you what to buy - they're people you once knew, or feel like you could know, offering up curated slices of their lives, It doesn't look like marketing. It looks like friendship. But here's the trick: It's neither. The rise of influencer culture has redefined what it means to belong. It's nolongerenough to participate; we must now perform. The purchase isn'tjust a transaction - it's adeclaration A Stanley cup isn't about hydration. It's about showing you know what's trending. A Dyson Airwrap isn't about styling hair - it's about signalling taste, alignment, and status, Products are no longer tools; they're identities. To be seen is to be affirmed. And to be affirmed, we must first consume.
