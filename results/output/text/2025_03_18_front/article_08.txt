China rising in Indo-Pacific, India and New Zealand seal defence pact

PMs welcome launch of FTA talks, discuss security cooperation

SIGNALLING DEEPER strategic convergence in the face of an assertive China in the Indo-Pacific region, India and New Zealand signed a defence cooperation pact Monday and agreed to step up dialogue to enhance maritime safety. The agreementwas inked af ter Prime Minister Narendra Modi met Prime Minister of New Zealand Christopher Luxon who is in Indiaon afive-day visit. The two leaders also discussed the issue of Khalistan separatist activities in New Zealand and India flagged its concern about "anti-Indiaactivities by some illegal dements". After the bilateral talks, Modi said both sides decided to strengthen and institutionalise defence and security partnership and aroadmap will be prepared for cooperation inthe defence industry sector. Modi said India and New Zealand support a free, open and secure Indo-Pacific. "We believe inthe policy of development, not expansionism," he said, inan oblique reference to China's aggressive behaviour in the Indo-Pacific region. Luxon said Modi and he discussed a challenging strategic outlook in the Indo-Pacific. "I reiterated our strong commitment to address shared concemsover our respective interests in contributing t a prosperous Indo-Pacific," he said. According to the joint statement, the Prime Ministers "recognised that we face an increasingly uncertain and dangerous world. They noted that, as maritime nations, Indiaand New Zealand have a strong and common interest in an open, inclusive, stable and prosperous Indo-Pacific, where the rules-based intemational orderis upheld".
