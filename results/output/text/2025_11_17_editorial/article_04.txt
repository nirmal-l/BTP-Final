Despite ample rains, Delhi has not hada single good air day this year. Itcameclose to having onein 2024, when the monitors registered 53. According to CPCB, an AQI below 50 poses no risk. In the first eight months of the year, Delhi also had 65 satisfactory air days - AQI of 50-100 ~ which according to CPCB could result in "minor discomfort" for sensitive individuals. In other words, before the emergency, thecity experienced at least 170 days when the AQI was "moderate" or poorer. What doesthat meanin healthterms? CPCB notes

that when an area has an AQI in the range of 100-200, sensitive people living there may experience breathing difficulties. The "general public" is affected when the 200 mark is breached, and beyond that health effects escalate. Square all this against the country's non-communicable disease burden, and it's apparent that Delhi's air quality is a health challenge even when the situation appears normal. This sensitivity remains elusive even though policy has begun to forge links between health and environment. It should be at the core of the most basic of the transparency criteria - AQ monitoring. Report after report, including in this newspaper, has underlined that many of Delhi's pollution monitors do not work, even during crisis times, or are placed at the wrong locations. Cities that have improved air quality - London, Beijing - have done so by treating it as a continuous challenge. In the last three years, for instance, the Breathe London Project has mapped air quality across the city using low costsensor systems. An important feature of London's long fight with poor air has been the inclination of administrators, especially aftertheGreat Smog of the 1950s, to take people along. Last Sunday, Delhi residents showed an awareness of the rights the SC has emphasised on. It's now the duty of administrators to take the next step - be vigilant through the year with a keen eye for people's health.

Indo-Pak trade deals

INDIA AND Pakistan will seek to expand trade and economicrelations through a number of proposals including joint ventures, resumption of private trade and participation in free trade zones of either country. Ideas and proposals on mutualcooperation were discussed by the visiting Pakistan Minister for Finance, Planning and Economic Affairs, Mahbub-ul-Haq, with Indian leaders. Itwas agreed that there should be at least one joint venture in each country, with both sides pooling their capital, experience and technology, as a "symbol of co-operation."
