The common thread in both stories? Packaged, flavoured drinks that claimed to be "ORS solutions". For years, Hyderabad-based paediatrician Dr Sivaranjini Santhosh has been collecting stories like these - heartbreaking accounts from parents, colleagues and anxious relatives who reach out to her through her social media platforms. Her aim has been to ensure that children with diarrhoea do not end upconsuming flavoured, sweet-ened beverages in place of the genuine Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS), a landmark discovery in medicine pioneered by Dr Dilip Mahalanabis. On Wednesday, the country's food regulator issued an order banning all beverages from using the term ORS in their trade-marked name, withdrawing an earlier order allowing the term with disclaimers. Over the last decade or so, several tetra-pack products claiming to be ORS have entered the market without actually adhering to the sugar and salt ratios needed fora person to effectively absorb the water they are drinking, Dr Santhosh told The Indian Express. Her journey started with educating people on social media around eight years ago - she saw children coming to her clinics who were severely dehydrated despite being given what the parents assumed was an ORS solution. But she knew that was not enough. These products were not only giving children excess sugar, they were actively harming their health.
