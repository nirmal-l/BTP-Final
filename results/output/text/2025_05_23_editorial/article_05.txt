TRUMP, BLACK & WHITE

His attempt to ambush President Cyril Ramaphosa flags challenges posed by White House he presides over ( ) HIEF AMONG THE many differences between diplomacy and reality TV is that the latter is built on "gotcha" moments and catchphrases. That the two can now be spoken of in the same breath speaks to the disruption of, and disregard for, the norms of diplomacy-as-usual by US President Donald Trump. Less than three months after he all but upended Washington's stance on the Russia-Ukraine conflict in a public spat with Volodymyr Zelenskyy at the White House, Trump tried to ambush South African President Cyril Ramaphosa with footage of questionable provenance - the four-minute video showed out-of-context clips of protestors and symbols. Trump, who has accused the South African government of aiding "white genocide", all but treated a visiting head of state as a contestant on The Apprentice. This diplomacy that sees summit-level meetings as an opportunity to score political points, and for posturing to a domestic base, has implications beyond the optics of the current moment. South Africa is indeed suffering froma crime wave. However,as data have shown, victims are overwhelmingly Black. When asked politely by Ramaphosa, Trump was unable to pinpoint exactly where and when the incidents in the video took place. In fact, he repeated the tropes of far-right conspiracy theorists, who have, without evidence, claimed mass killings of White South Africans and ignored the challenges of emerging from decades of colonialism and Apartheid. The attack on Ramaphosa is of a piece with the performative diplomacy of the current administration. The South African President tried to defuse the situation with a joke: "I am sorry don't have a plane to give you," alluding to the recent gift by Qatar to the US. Ramaphosa's conduct could provide a pointer on how to react when the leader of the world's pre-eminent superpower tries to turn a routine briefing into "good TV". This might be the way forward for leaders as they make their way in a world upended by the current administration in the US.
