17 deaths later, village in Jammu begins isolating victims' contacts

NEARLY 200 people from Baddal village moved toRajouri town for continuous monitoring, strict curbsonthemovement of those who remain in the village, and surveillance by newly installed CCTVcameras - these are among measures taken by the Jammu and Kashmir administration against the backdrop of 17 deaths and four hospitalisations of unexplained causes inthe village. Since December, 17 people from three families at Baddal vilage in Rajourihavedied, with authorities from J&Kand the Centre scrambling to figure out why. Just when officials expressed apprehensions that a baaii (waterreservoir) with traces of pesticides could be linked to the deaths, three more minors fromone family were admitted to the Government Medical College (GMC) Hospital in Rajouri with fever and vomiting. Union minister Jitendra Singh hassaid that preliminary results from a Council of Scientific and

Industrial Research ( CSIR) toxicology laboratoryin Lucknow indicated the presenceofatoxinin the bodies of those dead. "Now the toxin is being tested," he said, promising that the "mystery" would be cleared soon. Authoritieshave meanwhile moved around 200 people - the surviving members ofthe three families and their contacts - to the Nursing College in Rajouri. They are being monitored 24x7 by a dedicated team of doctors and medical staff. Police personne! have also been deployed at the nursing college and atthe hospital to ensure that no unauthorised person comes in or goes out of the isolation wards in the nursing college and GMC officialssaid.
