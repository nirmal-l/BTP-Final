On new ISRO chief's table: 100th launch this month, PSLV built by pvt sector

AFTER THE historic space docking experiment, the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) is getting ready for another milestone, its 100th launch, that is set to happen at the end of the month, This would be the first mission to be launched under the leadership of new ISRO Chairman V Narayanan who

took charge earlier this month. "The 100th launch of ISRO is planned for later this month. It would launch the NVS-02 satellite, which issecond of thesecondgeneration navigation satellites being deployed for India's NaviC constellation," NarayanantoldThe Indian Express in aninterview. NaviC, or Navigation with Indian Constellation, is the operational name for the Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System, IRNSS, which is a constellation of seven satellites providing positioning, navigation and timing services over the Indian region and its immediate

neighbourhood. The satellites are deployed in the geasynchronous orbits. First launched in July 2013, the IRNSS constellation wascompleted in 2018, but not all the satellites have been functional. The first set of satellites are now being replaced with NVS series of satellites that have more advanced features, and carry a moreaccurate Rubidium atomic clock for precise positioning and navigation. The first satellite in thisseries NVS-01 was launched in May 2023. NVS-02is the second in the series. These second-generation satellites havea mission life of 12 years. Narayanan said the launchof NVS-02 was another important step forward towards quick commercidlisation and adoption of India's ownindigenously-built navigation system. He said another important event he was looking forward to in the immediate future was the launch of private ly-built PSLV rocket. PSLV is ISRO's most prolific and reliable rocket, having made more than 60 flights since itsinduction in 1994. ISROis now getting the private industry to manufacture this rocket. 'The PSLV completely manufactured by the industry is inthe final phase. We will soon see a launch of that. We are going tw see increased participation of the private sector in space. The Prime Minister has rolled out reformsin the space sector, and itisnow our responsibility to hand-hold the private industry," Narayanan said. The ISRO Chairman also spoke about other major under-takings of the space agency, including the development of NGLV, the next generation launch vehicle with which he himself has been closely associated, and the upcoming Gaganyaan and Chandrayaan-4 missions.

"NGLY has a totally different kind of capability (compared to ISRO's current rockets). It will be 91 metreshigh almostastall as a 30-storey building, asagainst43 metres for (GSLV) Mk Ill rocket. It will have the capability ofcarrying 30,000 kgs tolow earth orbit. Our current capacity with the Bahubali Mk Ill rocket is only around 8,500 kgs. When you look at our future programmes, we will need to have very high capability rockets," he said. CONTINUEDON PAGE2
