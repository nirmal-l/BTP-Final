IN CRICKET, AS much as in any other chapter in history, characters appear for brief periods, make their statement and fade away into posterity. However, there are some characters who leave behind an indelible impression not merely by virtue of the runs they score or the number of wickets they take but by the revolution they create. Virat Kohli is the fourth-highest Indian run-getter in Test matches. He may not enjoy the sobriquet of "Master Blaster" or "the Wall", but as King Kohli, he introduced a whole new culture of physical fitness, dietary discipline and mental toughness. Test cricketers were at best seen chasing the ball or attempting to stop it heading towards the boundary with their legs. All-time greats like Vivian Richards or Kapil Dev were never seen diving, let alone swooping down, and, in one smooth motion, collecting the ball and throwing it straight into the wicket keeper's gloves. His retirement from Test cricket has dismayed young-sters across the country.
