Ukraine, between terrible war and humiliating peace

O COUNTRY deserves the fate of Ukraine. Its territory is under siege, its people have endured prolonged suffering, and its leadership is under pressure to accept a peace plan that largely favours Russia. Ukraine stands as a poignant reminder of the perils associated with relying on external support for conflict and security. Ukraine is incurring heavy losses at the frontline and cannot resist the mounting Russian pressure. It is facing manpower and material shortages against the intensifying Russian assaults. Russian troops have besieged the city of Pokrovsk, an industrial hub, where Ukrainian forces had fortified themselves. This city is crucial to Ukraine's military supply chain, given its extensive rail and road network connectivity. Russia aims to capture the entire Donbas region, comprising the Luhansk and Donetsk provinces. Inits latest phase of negotiations, the Donald Trump team, led by Secretary Marco Rubio and Steve Witkoff, is meeting with the Ukrainian team, led by Rustem Umerov, in Florida. The initial Trump peace proposal, known as the 28-point peace deal, has been revised under European pressure. The new proposal is likely to offer some security guarantees to Ukraine. The guarantee will certainly not be NATO membership, but the West can provide a formal commitment to Ukraine thatthe latter willbe protected from a future Russian assault. The European countries insist on Ukraine's right to protect its sovereignty and keep open the possibility of deploying European troops on Ukrainian territory. However, Russiais unlikely to agree to the deployment of European defence forces in Ukraine. Another sticking point is the issue of Donbas. Russia wants Ukraine to accept dejure the entire region of Donbas as its territory. Russiacontrols 90 per centofthe Donbas territory, and the rest remains with Ukraine. For Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, the withdrawal of troops and acceptingthe loss of the whole of Donbas is a dangerous proposition, as it would amount to the surrender of its territory. His regime, which has become highly vulnerable after corruption charges, will face stiff resistance from the opposition parties. Ukraine's chief negotiator, Andriy Yermak, was replaced due to allegations of corruption. Umerov is Ukraine's new negotiator in Florida. The survival of Zelenskyy's regime depends on continued European financial and military support. Thetragedy of Ukraine is that itcannot match the Russian firepower, and if the war continues, it will lose more territory. On the other hand, no peace proposal can retrieve the territory that Ukraine has lost to Russia.
