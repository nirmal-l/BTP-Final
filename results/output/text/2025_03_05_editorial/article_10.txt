SAJJAN KUMAR

THE BAHUJAN SAMAJ Party (BSP) and its leader, Mayawati, signify the once ascendant Dalit discourse that has been in terminal electoral decline since 2014 in Uttar Pradesh. Of all the reasons cited forthe BSP' sdipping fortunes, Mayawati's autocratic leadership styleand refusalto declarea worthy successorare perceived as two prime determinants. However, once we analyse the BSP question from the party workers' perspective, the picture changes diametrically. Their faith in Mayawati remains constant. This emerges most starkly in the back-dropof the predicament of Akash Anand inside the party. Being the nephew of Mayawati, Anand, 30, hasbeen perceived as her potential successor at a time when the coresupportbase of the party facesa formidable challenge from another young anddynamic leader, Chandrashekhar Azad. Anand wasdeclared the national coordinator of the BSP after the 2019 Lok Sabha election. In December 2023, Mayawati formally announced himas her heir. An MBA graduate from the UK, Anand heralded anew phaseof politics inside the BSP by appearing to be more relatable to Dalityouths who want the party to shift from the centrality of self-respect to the politics of aspiration. He has been vocal, speaks extempore, is at ease in both English and Hindi, and brought new elements like the padyatra to the party. Hence, the new heir wasn'tjust more relatable to Gen-Z Dalits; he also sought to structurally readjust the party.
