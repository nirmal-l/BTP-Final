General no longer so general: 36 hours on board an Amrit Bharat train

Seats with air-spring suspension to vacuum toilets and LED display - The Indian Express boards an Amrit Bharat, one of 50 upgraded express trains for migrant routes

SANJAY KUMAR, 38, puts train travel in perspective - no romance of the rails, just pure strategy. "Agar aap dabdaba nahi

dikhaiyega toh baithane ko bhi nahi milega (If you don't show your might, you won't even geta place to sit). This is the reality of the general class compartment." Sitting by an open window, legs folded, he says, "This time, I am lucky. I usually don't get the window seat... I don't even get to sit."

Sanjay, a mechanic from Gopalganj in Bihar, is on board

the Saharsa-Lokmanya Tilak Terminus Amrit Bharat, one ofa series of 50 affordable express trains for general- and sleeper-class passengers that the government plans torun on routes that see high movement of migrant workers.
