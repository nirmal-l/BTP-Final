The electoral supply chain encompasses not just physical materials but digital systems, energy backups, health protocols, and legal safeguards. Every booth receives EVMs and VVPATs (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trails), signage, seals, indelible ink, voter slips, PPE kits (post- 2020),and sometimes tents, chairs, and water canisters. Over 4,00,000 vehicles, 1,700 airsorties, and 135 special trains were deployed in 2024. Inareas unreachable by road, donkeys, camels, elephants and helicopters take over. EVMs are stored in GPS-monitored strongrooms with triple-layered security. Each polling booth is manned by five trained officials inside and five outside. They undergo orientation in machine handling, crowd control, and legal codes. Election duty, though temporary, is supported by a vast training-and-deployment pipeline akin to a seasonal work-force on a national scale. Real-time dashboards, turnout tracking apps, encrypted VPNs, satellite uplinks, and mobile networks support live data flow. Media centres run 24x7 during polls. To ensure poll purity, the cVIGIL app processes complaints, most resolved within 100 minutes. In off-grid booths, solar panels, battery banks, and diesel generators ensure that voting continues without interruption. Fuel dispatches are pre-planned. CAPF, state police, and Home Guards secure over 1,60,000sensitive booths. EVMs are sealed with barcodes and dual locks. CCTV footageis monitored live; tamper-evidence is embedded at every step. Millions of disposableitems - masks, gloves, water bottles, paper forms - are collected, segregated, and handed over to municipal agencies or recyclers. Each polling station is not just equipped to conduct an election, but to leave no trash. What's striking is how closely the Indian electoral process mirrors modern supply-chain thinking: Just-in-time deployment; reverse logistics in EVM retums, audits, and redeployments; quality control via mock polls
