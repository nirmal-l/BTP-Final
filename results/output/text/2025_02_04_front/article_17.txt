MINUTES PAST midnight, on August 15, 1947, the echoes of Mahatma Gandhi ki jai and Bharat Mata kijai reverberated through the Cannanore (now Kannur) jail where Communist leader A K Gopalan, then 43, was

in solitary confinement He had secretly kept anational flag with him for the occasion. At dawn, he hoisted the Tricolour where other prisoners gathered to hear him speak. Barely four or five minutes into his speech, the jail authorities topped him. Aday later, accused of stirring up the people "against His Majesty the Emperor', Gopalan (or AKG, as is popularty known) was produced before a magistrate in Calicut (now Kozhikode) and charged with sedition. He was released on October 12, 1947, butjustover amonthlater, he was detained again under the colonial laws that were still in place in the newly independent nation.
