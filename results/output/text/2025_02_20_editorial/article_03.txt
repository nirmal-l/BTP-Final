Theoretically, this is agreat opportunity for Delhi, a realisation that a balance must be struck between the twohalves Butin practice, as the Centre for Policy Research's Cities of Delhi project showed, Delhi's inequality is actively produced by four things. First is the way in which state power is organised in favour of the middle class. The second is that the middle-class modemist imagination relies on the presence of the poor for services without wanting to make them visible. There is still a fear that the modemism by which Delhi's success will be judged involves constant displacement, evictionand marginalisationof the poor. The third is a genuine problem for most Indian cities - buying modernismon the cheap, with no mechanisms for costing the externalities of development.
