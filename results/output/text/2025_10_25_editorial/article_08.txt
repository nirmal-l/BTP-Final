very "Hindi", but with knowledge about the culture in other parts of the country, like Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh. He came from an astonishing family - his sister Ila Arun sang ballads, his brother Prasoon Pandey made beautiful films. But they were all very rooted in the customs, ethos and festivities of India; they knew the country so well. And you could see the beautiful aspects of India coming through in Piyush's work - whether he was doing advertising for Asian Paints, whether it was the long-running campaign for Fevicol, those great ads for Cadbury. He even did the initial ad for the IPL. Piyush's sensibilities were quintessentially Indian. And that changed advertising in the country for good - everybody started thinking in terms of "India". Later, in the south, the thinking switched to Tamil, Telugu, etc., but thanks to Piyush's work, weall started thinking in Hindi. I'll give you an example of how "English" advertising used to be. In my early days, when working on a campaign, the art director would want to know if it would appear in the Sunday Observer. This was so that his social circle and his family could see the ad.
