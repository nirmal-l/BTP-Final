LAW AND DISORDER

In aftermath of violence in Murshidabad over Wadf Act, for West Bengal government, peace, not politics, must be priority \ )' 4 EST BENGAL CHIEF Minister Mamata Banerjee has appealed to people in the state to maintain calm after protests against the Waqf Act tumed violent in Murshidabad district on Friday. The primary responsibility to restore peace, and to maintain law and order, rests with the government Asit takes political stand on acontentiousissue, however, important lines are in danger of being blurred in Bengal. Emotions have been running high in the wake of Parliament's approval of the contentious legislation. Murshidabad is also known to be a communally sensitive district where protests against the Citizenship (Amendment) Act had tumed violentsix years ago. The districtsaw communal violence around thetime ofRam Navami in April last year, and again in November. This time, the TMC government failed to anticipatethe trouble, and has much toanswer for. For more than aday, the law-and-order machinery seemed to be flailing as arsonists ran amok, leading to the loss of three lives. Houses and shops were vandalised and looted. It required the Calcutta High Court's intervention and the deployment of central forces for a semblance of order to be restored. Banerjee's TMC has been staunchly critical of the Waqf Act. But when aCM ofa state opposes a law passed by Parliament, she must scrupulously take the constitutional route. Banerjee has done the right thing in signalling that she will protect minority interests. She would also be entitled to weigh legal options against the Waqf Act. But instead of letting the due process unfold, Banerjee has given in to the temptation of political grandstanding. Her announcement that West Bengal will not implement the new law can be seen as playing to the gallery. The stance has much to do with the brand of politics she has adopted since upstaging the Left Front 14 years ago. The West Bengal CM hasnot been seen to be averse to pandering to hardline elements in both communities. The incidents last week should make the West Bengal CM reflect, and apply correctives - several parts of the country have witnessed protests in the wake of the waqf amendment, but nowhere have theagitations tumed as violent as they did in Murshidabad. People belonging to all communities in the district are reportedly in the grip of fear, and several families have fled to neighbouring Malda. The state government must devote its energies to rebuilding lives and restoring trust among communities.
