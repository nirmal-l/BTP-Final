WhatLohia argued for was a comprehensive policy of preferential opportunities. While invoking his demand for 60 per cent quota for backwards - captured in his slogan "pichhada pave sau me saath" - it isoften forgotten that, for Lohia, "pichhada" included OBCs, Dalits and Adwvasis as well as women. He saw gender injustice as the primary one among the six forms of injustice that called for saptakranti, seven revolutions. He maintained that women, cutting across caste and class, were equivalent to Shudras and were entitled to preferential opportunities.
