Insulate Dhaka ties from short-term politics

HE PROTESTS at Indian diplomatic outposts, including the High Commission in Dhaka, are concerning. They also carry a disquieting portent for a bilateral relationship that was, until recently, arguably Delhi's greatest success in the neighbourhood. The demand for the repatriation of Sheikh Hasina by the group marching under the banner of "July Oikya" and the anti-India rhetoric of National Citizen Party leader Hasnat Abdullah, threatening to provide shelter and support to separatists from northeastern states, less than two months before the general elections, show just how deeply politicised bilateral ties have become. Delhi has summoned the Bangladesh High Commissioner and made its concerns known. However, the leadershin at the highest level in both Cotintries must prioritise the need to repair the relationship. With the benefit of hindsight, it is clear that India underestimated the depth of support for thestudent-led uprising and the anger against Sheikh Hasina. During the protests and in their aftermath, certain sections on both sides of the border further polarised the conversation by invoking historical socialand communal fissures in the Subcontinent. In the age of 24/7 news and WhatsApp forwards, irresponsible rhetoric cannot butaffect the tenor ofties with animportant neighbour. Onits part, theinterim government in Dhaka has done little to bridge the domestic political divide. As Sajeeb Wazed Joy, Sheikh Hasina's son, under-lined in an interview to this newspaper, the Awami League has been a major political force in Bangladesh since its formation, andthe ban on theparty fromcontesting will castashadowover the election. By excluding the Awami League from the political process, theinterim government is repeating Hasina's mistakes. It is important for Delhi and Dhaka to recall the realities of geography, history, culture, geopolitics and economics that cannot be wished away. Both countries have made strides in trade, tackling insurgencies, sharing river waters and policing the shared 4,096-km land border. India must communicate to the people of Bangladesh as muchas its government that the bilateralstory isaboutiwosocieties, regardiess ofthe government in power. Delhi must becareful not to takepolitical sidesin Dhaka. The political class in Bangladesh, foritspart, needsto show maturity ahead oftheelection. Inthe longterm, both countries have much to gain by building back the relationship.
