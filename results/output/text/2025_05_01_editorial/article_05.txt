draws attention toa stalemate waiting to be broken in the state TT HE DECISION TO impose President's Rule in Manipur on February 13 followed a failure to reach a consensus within the BJP on who would replace N Biren Singh as chief minister. Singh had resigned four days earlier, after dissident BJP MLAsstarted putting pressure on him to step down. On the one hand, his exit was seen as a necessary step for anew start, while also being an implicit admission of his inability to curtail the violence between the Meiteis and the Kukis. On the other hand, the BJP in Manipur was, reportedly, against President's Rule, arguably because that would mean giving the Centre a greater role in the state. The opposition to its prolongation has become more pronounced, with a group of 21 Manipur MLAs of the NDA submitting letters to Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Home Minister Amit Shah on April 29, asking for a popular government to be installed in the state. The letter claims that even after almost three months of President's Rule, there have been "no visible actions to bring peace and normalcy in the state". The fact is that in twoyears of conflict, at least250 people have died and thousands continue to languish in relief camps across the state. It is true that there has been alull in the violence between the Meiteis and the Kukis. Barring some clashes between security forces and civilians, an uneasy peace has prevailed in most parts of the state since the escalation in November last year in Imphal West, Bishnupur and Jiribam districts. But Manipur is far from a return to normalcy. There has been nomeaningful political dialogue. Members of both communities still cannot return home, and live without fear or prejudice. Highways remain blocked, and there has been minimal disarmament and demilitarisation, despite Governor Ajay Kumar Bhalla's ultimatum to militants, less than a week into President's Rule, to surrender their weapons. The situation demands a restoration of the political process that can bring all sides to the negotiating table. The larger goal is to restore trust between the Meitei and Kukicommunities, While a return of a popular government in the state is urgent and desirable, the Centre must also prioritise confidence-building measures and consult with all the stakeholders, The last thing Manipurneeds is a resurgence of gun violence and rioting that would undo the fragile calm of the past several weeks.
