THE BULLETS THAT echoed in the Baisaran Valley in Pahalgam shook India out of its slumber. The executions, perpetrated and claimed by The Resistance Front, an offshoot of Pakistan's Lashkar-e-Taiba, brought back memories of the Uri (2016) and Pulwama (2019) attacks, India's responses - surgical strikes on terrorist camps after the former, and air strikes on terrorist installations near Balakot after the latter - seemed as if they had brought down the levels of terrorism. But Pakistani terrorism reared its ugly head again. There are two reasons for this: One, if the terrorist movementis allowed to diedown, it will be very difficult to restart it. Two, it is a low-cost option for Pakistan to keep India on the boil as part of Operation Tupac, the proxy militancy conceived by General Ziaul Over the years, olive branches have been extended by successive Prime Ministers of India, from K Gujral to Narendra Modi, but every time the relationship appeared to be improving, it was always brought back tothe brink by Pakistan's deep state. India has practised restraint on many occasions, including after the Parliament attack in 2001 and the Mumbai attacks of November 26, 2008. So, India's actions turned a page with the surgical strike in 2016 and air strikes in 2019. The Baisaran Valley killings took the violence up a notch by singling out Hindu men and executing them in front of their family members and children. Inthis, Pakistan has invited trouble.
