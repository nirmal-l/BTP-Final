The stories are here, half-told, waiting. Tales from the colonial military hospital that received the dying from passing ships; modem tragedies of love, loss, and political drama. Aspiring authors, are you listening? Epitaph 6: "Gone underground, till tonight." By evening, the mourners leave and the gates close. The bats rise. Kanatte has a healthy share of ghost stories - men in white who vanish by the mortuary wall, women in saris who flutter under moonlight. The guards report resveem, ghost meetings, where figures coalesce and blur into the night air like an arrack-filled breath. Tombstones or swaying trees never took spooky shapes for me. find the night cemetery a picture of profound tranquillity. The air grows heavy with frangipani and damp earth, a quiet punctuated by the scurry of a civet or the leathery flap of a fruit bat's wings. The dead are wiser because they have run out of timeto be foolish. Epitaph 7: "if you're reading this, my story still has readers." Standing here, you realise Kanatte tells a story, too. A silent epic written in granite and grief, it insists that pluralism is not a lofty ideal but an inevitable reality. Do we really need to die to discover our common ground? With this, list my last future epitaph: "Deadline met. This column ends here."
