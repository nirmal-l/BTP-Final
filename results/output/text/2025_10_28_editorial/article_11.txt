DIPESH CHAKRABARTY, IN his book Provincialising Europe, questioned the universalisation of "modernity" as an idea. Since the 19th century, concepts like secularism, liberalism and modemity, and theirmarkers, have been mostly read as "neutral" and "natural." The socio-political contexts and the material referents that produced these abstract ideas gradually vanished, except when they were scrutinised by postcolonial scholars. Now, as Alstarts to shape socio-political discourse, one must stand against Western bias in the data that feeds its worldview, because today's bias could become tomorrow's "norm", at leastin terms of representation. Aranya Sahay's debut film, Humans in the Loop (2024), questions such bias in data labelling - the process of feeding data into Al - and reimagines training the technology in an Adivasi way. Set in aJharkhand village, the film follows the life of Nehma, a divorced Adivasi woman, who joins a data labelling centre working for a US-based company, so that she can get custody of her teenage daughter, Dhannu, and toddler, Guntu.
