"TARIFFS AND CUTS

RBI has lowered rates. Extent of further cuts will be shaped by how domestic growth-inflation evolve amid trade war

NITSFIRST meeting of the financial year, the RBI's monetary policy committee voted unanimously tolower the policy reporate by 25 basis points. The decision was along expected lines. The reporate now stands at 6 per cent. Alongside this, the MPC also voted to change the policy stance from "neutral" to "accommodative". This, as RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra elaborated, implies that in the absence of any shocks, the committee will consider only two policy actions - either maintaining the status quo or cutting interest rates further. The governor also committed to providing sufficient system liquidity. This signals an easy monetary policy regime to stimulate the economy. This rate cut comes amidst heightened global uncertainty following US President Donald Trump's imposition of tariffs on America's trading partners, Given the varied responses by countries - for instance, while China has retaliated, imposing tariffs on the US, others like Australia have ruled out responding in kind - the effects of Trump's policies remain uncertain, But the global economic outlook has become muddied. Against this backdrop, the RBI governor outlined the likely impact of tariffs on growth and inflation. On growth, he noted that tariff-related uncertainties could impact both investment and spending decisions of households and firms. They will also introduce trade friction, affecting both global trade and growth. As a consequence, the central bank has now lowered its forecast for growth this year, pegging growth at 6.5 per cent, down from 6.7 per cent earlier. This downward revision, the governor noted, "reflects the impact of global trade and policy uncertainties". Some analysts, however, expect growth to be even lower. On the other hand, while the outlook for food inflation has tumed "decisively positive", and crude oil prices have trended lower, the global environment has injected a degree of uncertainty. The RBI governor noted that while uncertainty could put pressure on the currency, leading to imported inflation, the slowdown in global growth may lead to a softening of commodity prices. The central bank has now projected inflation at 4 per cent this year, down from its earlier forecast of 4.2 per cent. It now expects inflation to average 3.75 percent in the first half of the year and 4.1 per cent in the second half. The governor has said that "there is now a greater confidence of a durable alignment of headline inflation with the target of 4 per cent over a 12-month horizon". This suggests the space for further policy easing. The question nowis to what extent. Some analysts are now expecting rates to be cut by another 50 basis points, which would bring the repo rate down to 5.5 per cent. However, there is uncertainty. The pace and the extent of further reductions in the interest rate will be determined by how the domestic growth-inflation dynamic evolves amid the ongoing trade war.
