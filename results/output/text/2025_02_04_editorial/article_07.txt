A weeklater, I'm in the GhennaiRiver Restoration Trust office, across from an irritated bureaucrat telling methat theywould not concretise the paths, notcut the trees Butofcourse: Concretehas been carried in, trees cut, theestuary closed for renovations. In me, anew gaze finds a new Chennai: Thisis, above all else, a waterscape in relentless negotiation with urbanity'simpulse to conquer, to want. In Odiyur, a lagoon resists a highway. In Kattupalli, acoast resists a megaport. In Ennore, a river and a creek stand where industries and power plants spill poison. Urbanity wins small battles. In annual floods and relentless erosion, water has its say. The casualties, regardless, are the same: Vulnerable communities, displaced or put outof work: marine and avian and insectlife, rendered breathless, habitat-less; acity at large, flood-prone, conscience heavy. Jask Chennai: Is it possible atall? For human impulses t co-exist with the rest of life?
