'THE HEDGEWAR MANTRA RSS continues to be inspired by his vision of patriotism and social responsibility

PRAVIN DABADGHAV

THIS YEAR'S VIJAYADASHAMI marks thecentenary of the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS). Gudi Padwa, which fell on Sunday and coincides with the birthday of Keshav Baliram Hedgewar, the founder of the RSS, holds special significance. One of the most defining aspects of Hedgewar's legacy is the "Sangh Mantra" he shared with thousands of swayamsevaks. It continues to shape the path of the organisation. Unwavering patriotism and exceptional organisational skills were among Hedgewar's remarkable qualities. Bom on on April 1, 1889, in Nagpur, he was driven by a desire to free India from British colonial rule from a young age. As a 16-year-old, he formed a group with his peers, reading newspapers such as Kaal, Kesari, Bhala, and Amrit Bazaar Patrika. At the same time, he was involved in thesaleand distribution of indigenous (swadeshi) goods. Hedgewar was a vocal advocate for India's freedom, famously chanting "Vande Mataram" at a Dussehra procession at Rampayali, where he also delivered a passionate speech. For this, he was expelled from school. After joining a private institution and completing his matriculation, he moved to Kolkata in 1910 to pursue medical studies, Kolkata offered Hedgewar an opportunity to engage directly with the Independence movement. Hereturnedto Nagpur in 1916 after completing his medical education and became involved in social work. He assisted in organisingvolunteers atthe Nagpur Congress session in 1920, and joined the Non-

Cooperation Movement launched by the Congress, His impassioned sneeches led to his arrest in May 1921. In 1923-24, he began publishing Swatanya, the first daily newspaperin the Central Provinces and Berar. In 1930, Mahatma Gandhi called for civil disobedience, and Hedgewar participated in the forest satyagraha in Vidarbha, following Gandhi's Salt Satyagraha. OnJuly 21, 1930, he led a satyagraha in the Lohara forest near Yavatmal. He was arrested and sentenced that

same day. By this time, the RSS had already begun its work. Hedgewar was troubled by the reality that asmall group of Englishmen could dominate avastnation like India. The root cause, he concluded, was the disorganisation and disunity within Indian society, particularly among Hindus. Tocounter this, Hed gewar envisioned an awakened and well-organised Hindu society. He founded the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh on Vijayadashami, Sentember 27, 1925, Hedgewar's vision was simple but powerful: Swayamsevaks would gather at shakhas for an houreach day forexercises, games, and drills, The goal was not only physical fitness but cultivating a sense of patriotism and social responsibility. This was the essence of the "Sangh Mantra". Today, the RSS runs more than 89,000 service projects across the country, demonstrating the vast scope and profound impact of its work. In a letter to swayamsevaks on May 2, 1935, Hedgewar wrote: "We haveundertaken the great responsibility of national work. The

high goal of the RSS must be proven through real practice, and we must demonstrate to people that principles must be put into action... Discipline is the foundation of our organisation, and itis upon this foundation that we must build our immense structure." As the RSS reaches its 100th year, Hedgewar's vision remains deeply relevant. The RSS's commitment to environmental awareness (paryavaran) resonates today as sustainability becomes a global priority. Hedgewar's advocacy of self-reliance (swabodh) counters the global over-dependence on external resources and fosters economic resilience. Kutumb prabodhan (family values) addresses the challenges faced by modern family structures. Finally, his focus on nagari shishtachar (civic sense and duty) is crucial in aworld where ethical behaviour and social responsibility are often sidelined. Economically, Hedgewar championed the conceptof self-reliance or swadesh. This idea has regained substantial relevance today, as economies worldwide grapple with supply chain vulnerabilities and excessive reliance on extemal sources for basic goods and services. The RSS, through its century-long journey, continues to stand asa testament to the practical application of his ideas. Hedgewar's vision remains an essential guiding force in addressing the challenges of today's world.

The writer is the prant karyavah of the Rashtriva Swayamsevak Sangh, Paschim Maharashtra Prant
