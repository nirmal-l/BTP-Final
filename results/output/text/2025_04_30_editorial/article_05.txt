WHERE (SOODNESS LIVES Future belongs to those who lead with integrity - in business, politics, philanthropy

RADHA GOENKA

WHILE THE BRUTAL terrorist ambush in Kashmiris freshin our minds, whereinnocent lives were lost and the region's fragile calm shattered; as we scroll through headlines of natural disasters, violence, and impulsive political decisions that disrupt the lives of millions, it's natural to wonder, is goodness dead? The answer is no. Tocounter every alarming report on global warming, there are clean energy and climate innovations that are helping us achieve our net-negative targets. For everyindividual defying reason in power, there are leaders doing the right thing, away from the spotlight, righting the wrongs of the few around them. Goodness hasn't vanished; it doesn't always make news, Society is broadly shaped by three forces: Commercial interests, political motivations, and humanitarian intentions, For the most part, the firsttwo are seenas exploitative, while service and charity are viewed as balancing their excesses. But the lines aren't so neatly drawn. Let's look at business. Most companies begin with a simple, often noble, goal. People needed a better way to get from point A to point B: Enter ride-sharing companies. We needed better housing: Cue the infrastructure boom. Businesses were born to meet needs. But somewhere along the way, the original intentgets lost. Founders stepaway, passion gives way to profit, and conglomerates runbyquarterly spreadsheets take over. Politics follows asimilar arc. Noone enters

public life unless they genuinely care about making a difference. It'sa calling - toserve, to shape policy, to bring change. But politics can become afamily legacy focused on preserving power and accumulating wealth. If you look at the world of philanthropy, it is often seen as a force meant to correct the wrongs of the other two worlds - but it only exists because of them. Most philanthropic efforts are funded by businesses or individuals who have eamed their wealth through commerce. Government support enables them to expand their efforts. Soif business, politics, and philanthropy all begin with the same seed of doing good, why can't they operate from that shared core instead of opposing corners? What ifwe stopped seeing them as separate domains, and instead recognised their common purpose: Service to society? That point of overlap - the intersection of profit, policy,and purpose - is where the real magic lies. It's where goodness lives. It's also where we might just find our collective ikigai. There are examples where this alignment hasworked, Take Intel, which in 1990 created alow-power chip that made portable laptops possible. The innovation was rooted not ina chase for profit, but in the founder's desire to save energy. His passion for the environment led to a breakthrough that unlocked a new computing era and billions in revenue. Or Patagonia, a brand that put the planet first and still became amulti-billion-dollaroutdoor giant, Closer to home, Amul began as a farmerled cooperative and grew into a Rs 55,000- crore dairy powerhouse by putting peopiebefore profit. These stories remind us that doing good isn'tjust idealistic, its deeply effective. Thereare countries where politics stilloperates from aplace of purpose. In New Zealand, Jacinda Ardern's government introduced a wellbeing budget that prioritised mental health, child welfare, and climate resilience over pure economic growth. Denmark combines high-trust governance with green innovation. And, in Bhutan, policies are guided not by GDP, but by Gross National Happiness, balancing development with environmental and cultural preservation. These models snow that governments can lead with empathy and vision without compromising prosperity. Soyes, the world can feel broken. But even in the face of terror, we see acts of courage, compassion and solidarity that defy despair. In these times, goodness is not gone; it's just quieter, It lives in those who rush to help, rebuild and lead with integrity. If we can recogniseand investin that quiet goodness, whether in people, politics or purpose-driven business, we may just find that hope isn't lost. It's wait-ingtobeamplified. I willgo so farasto say that if you are looking to invest ina company or a person, look for the goodness, the ikigai. Success is sure to follow.
