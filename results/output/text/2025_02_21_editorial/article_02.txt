The new welfarism in India, picked up by AAP and other parties across differentstates, is new in two senses One, there is the temporary provisioning ofa "safety net" against what maybe termed "market failure". For instance, a lump sum given every month to every woman from the economically weaker sections isan adaptation of welfare to the requirements of the markets. Two, it operates beyond the NGO-centric welfare measures and is characterised by universal welfare benefits. In both processes, however, this welfare gets nationalised instead of socialised. The problem with such welfarism is that it doesn't lead to the empowennent of the citizen; rather, it increases the public provisioning of private goods and cash benefits and enhances dependency. A girl child in Delhi, for example, would want robust democratic institutions and sustainable social infrastructure to enable her to exercise her socio-economic rights as freely as any other citizen in the long run. Wecan measure the advancement of the welfare state in terms of state welfare recipients or through some form of market-related provision, such as private insurance and occupational welfare programmes, but we can hardly measure the success of the welfare state. The real problem, therefore, is the failure to effect a fully "institutional" welfare Sate,in particular, one that addresses the issue of full employment. For the new government in Delhi, the real challenge will beto address these limitations. Govemments seldom think on a long-term basisand are caught intheirown web of "freebie politics", doing more harm than good by making the citizen passive, dependent and disempowered over time. In its manifesto, the BJP laid out an ambitious roadmap for the city's future, giving financial benefits to almost all sections of

society in one way or the other. In the fiscal year 2024-25, there isa projection of revenue decline from taxes, non-tax sources and central receipts. With more than two-thirds of revenue being utilised in the establishment and salary expenses, there seems tobe the possibility of slippage into deficit for the firsttime. Given such financial conditions, the exchequer is expected to face a heavy burden of financial accountability. For the new government, revenue distribution might be tight, so it may have to either divert funds from capital expenditure orother sectors to finance all the new welfare schemes, or it may have to resort to help from the Centre. It remains to be seen how the new dispensation works on the anthmetic to get revenues for the promised schemes in its budget. The citizens of Del hi have voted emphatically for change. The BJP has notched upa two-third majority in the assembly, indicating wide support not just from the disenchanted middleclass Thereisa rising expectation from the new government for better city governance, particularty in the areas of pollution, safety and security, health and education and employment opportunities.
