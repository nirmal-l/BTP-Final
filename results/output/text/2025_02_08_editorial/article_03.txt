RBI lowers interest rates as economicmomentum slows. Inflation projections suggest space for further easing

N FRIDAY, THE RBI'smonetary policy committee cutinterest rates by25 basis points, joining the list of central banksacross the world who have moved toease rates. The benchmark repo rate stands at 6.25 percent. The decision of the committee - this was the first time the MPChad met under thenew RBI govemor, Sanjay Malhotra - was unanimous. Alongside, the committee voted to continue with the neutral policy stance.The MPC's decision to adopt aless restrictive policy clearly indicates that growth considerations now outweigh concems over inflation. The momentum ini ihe econdiniy dipped sharply in the second quarter, with GDP growth coming in at 5.4 per cent. The first advance estimates have pegged growth at 6.4 per cent for the full year However, the pick-upin the second halfof the year does not seem to be robust. The RBI has noted "early cor porate results for Q3 indicate a mild recovery in themanufacturing sector." Greater clarity will emerge in the coming weeks and months. Thecentral bank now expects the economy to grow at 6.7 percent in 2025-26 - thisisat the higher end of the Economic Survey's projections, which had pegged growth t range between6.3 and 6.8 per cent Inflation, however, is now less of a concern with subdued core inflation and food prices expected to moderate. As per the RBI's latest projections, the consumer price index is projected to come down from 4.8 percent this year t0 4.2 per cent nextyear. It says that food prices should witness softening due to "good kharif production, winter-easing in vegetable prices and favourable rabi crop prospects". The decision to cut rates comes only days after the central bank announced its plan to inject liquidity into thebanking system through open-market operations, variable rate repo auctions and dollar/rupee buy/sell swap auctions. In hiscomments, the RBI govemor said that the central bank would continue to monitor liquidity and financial market predicaments, and "take appropriate measures to ensure orderly liquidity conditions". As per analysts, this should allow for "smoother transmisson of rate cuts inthemoney market". The MPC's decision to lower rates comes after the govemment cut personal income taxes in the Union budget, a measure that is expected to boost household consumption, providing fillip t the broader economy.
