Citing 'constitutional, moral duty', judges from SC, Manipur HC visit relief camps

WITH THOUSANDS of peoplein Manipur close to completing two years displaced from their homes, judges from the Supreme Court and Manipur High Court led by Justice BR Gavai visited relief camps in Churachandpur and Bishnupur on Saturday. Even in their visit, the deep fault lines persisting inthe state were evident, with Justice N Kotiswar Singh - a judge from the Supreme Court delegation from the Meitei community - and two Meitei Manipur High Courtjudges, A Bimol Singh and A Guneshwar Sharma, skipping the visit to Kuki-Zo majority Churachandpur The judges visited a relief camp eachin Churachandpur and Meitei-majority Bishnupur as part of a delegation from the

National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) and distributed relief materials such as mattresses and health cards. They also virtually inaugurated medicalcamps and 265 legal clinics in 109 locations across the state. "One of the most vulnerable sections of the societycomprises intemally displaced persons due tovarious circumstances beyond their contro}. Many individuals and families have found themselves uprooted from their houses, struggling to rebuild their lives. It is our moral and constitutional duty to ensure thatthey arenot leftbehind. the legal aid will also play a crucial role in ensuring that displaced individuals receive their rightful entitlements, be it in matters of identity documentation, property rights or compensation claims," said Justice Gavai, who is the executive chairman of NALSA, in Churachandpur on Saturday.
