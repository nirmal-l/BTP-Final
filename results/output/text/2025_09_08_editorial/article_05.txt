The renewed terms of the SoO tighten compliance conditions: Mandatory physical verification, photo identity cards, and reporting to local police stations wheneverthe cadres leave the camps. It will not be easy, as is evident from signs of discontent among both the Meiteis and the Kukis. In the valley, COCOMI, an Imphal-based Meitei organisation, condemned the renewal of the SoO agreement, alleging that it legitimises the militant groups. Inthe hills, the Kuki-Zo Council issued a statement claiming that NH-02 was never blocked and insisting on maintaining the "sanctity of buffer zones." Meanwhile, the United Naga Council has announced a "trade embargo" beginning September 8 to protest the fencing of the India-Myanmar border and the scrapping of the Free Movement Regime. This could jeopardise the progress made on free movement, as NH-02 runs through the Naga-dominated Senapati district. If Manipur is to break free from militia-driven politics, the new agreement will need to serve as the basis for a political dialogue that addresses the deeper issues sustaining the conflict. The PM's visit comes at a critical moment, when the prospect of resolution exists, but only if the opportunity is seized.
